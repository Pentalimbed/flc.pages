# The Rules

Originally proposed by Damian J. Blättler, **anchor structures** is a method to analyse the Parisian modernist repertoire of composers *e.g.* Ravel and Debussy, defined as

> the vertical-domain component of a novel chord that allows for its participation in tonal progression.

Simply put, for all the fancy unorthodox chords & voicings the Parisian modernist composers have used, certain passages within their pieces exhibit almost *tonal* sonorities, as if the chords are mirrors of their traditional/functional/tonal counterpart.
Their functions, as Blättler conjectures, are signified by the **anchor structures** within the voicing, and are enhanced by additional **adorning tones** that colour the harmony.
Blättler suggested rules of what structures & tones facilitate (or undermine) the tonal resemblance, as follows:

1. <span style="color:darkolivegreen">**First-order anchor structures**</span> are 2-note patterns, including 5ths, minor and major 7ths.
2. <span style="color:steelblue">**Second-order anchor structures**</span> are 3-note patterns, including
    - consonant triads in inversion,
    - seventh chords in inversion with the omission of either the chordal third or fifth,
    - the diminished triad and its inversions.
3. When there are multiple anchor structures, the priorities are
    - Structures closer to the bass > those above.
    - 2-note structures > 3-note structures.
4. <span style="color:firebrick">**Minor 2nd & minor 9th**</span> *etc.* could introduce too much roughness, that prevents hearing a sonorous composite chord.
    - An exception is the minor 9th above the bass of a chord with a two-note anchor structure.
5. <span style="color:firebrick">**Consecutive whole steps**</span> might make the chord sound more like scales, which is undesirable.
6. No adjacent tones are more than an octave apart.
7. Ultimately, if it sounds correct, then all above don't matter.

For more nuanced explanation with examples, please refer to the [original papers](#references).

# The Tool

Here's a simple web tool that can help analyse any chord voicing (within 3 octaves), and identify anchoring & destablizing structures.

Add/remove notes by clicking on the keys, and all identified structures will be shown to the right, at the row of the root of the structure.
<span style="color:darkolivegreen">First-order structures</span> are green, <span style="color:steelblue">second-order structures</span> are blue, while <span style="color:firebrick">potential rule violations</span> are coloured red.
Hovering above the structures symbols will show you their notes, as well as what functional chord they may replace.

I didn't check for bass-9th structures or rule 6. So please use your own judgment, cheers.
